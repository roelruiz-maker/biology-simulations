Your investigation

From lamp distance to a measurable gas sample.

Use a virtual pondweed apparatus to investigate the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis. Move the lamp, allow the plant to adjust, collect gas for a fixed time and measure the gas column in a capillary tube.

This replaces the unavailable wet-lab apparatus. You will practise careful measurement, repeat sampling and recording raw data. You will use your results for calculations, graphing and evaluation on paper after this stage.

What you measure
The length of collected gas in a tube of known internal diameter. This is a proxy for oxygen released by the pondweed. In a real plant, respiration and dissolved oxygen affect the amount of gas collected.

This is a teaching model with prepared, code-specific readings. It is not a measurement from a living plant. No account or installation is required. Your progress is stored locally in this browser for this website.

Prepare the apparatus

Understand the real-lab setup.

  1. Prepare the solution. A 500 cm³ beaker contains 250 cm³ distilled water and 10 cm³ of 1 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydrogencarbonate solution.
  2. Position the pondweed. A fresh cut at about 45° is held inside the submerged microburette bulb. A paper clip weighs down the intact end.
  3. Prime the microburette. A 5 cm³ syringe draws solution into the bulb and capillary. Clear the initial air before collecting a sample. The screw clip controls the sealed connection.
  4. Set the light. The same lamp is moved along a ruler. Room lights are off. Other conditions remain fixed in this model.

Fixed conditions in this model

Same pondweed shoot, solution, lamp and capillary throughout. Internal capillary diameter = 1.00 mm. Temperature = 25 °C (a model setting).

After each change of distance: 5 simulated minutes to adjust. Each gas collection: 5 simulated minutes. Three collections at each distance.

At the default 30× speed, 5 simulated minutes takes 10 seconds. This changes waiting time only. It does not change the five-minute collection period or the readings.
